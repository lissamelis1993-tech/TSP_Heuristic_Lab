Instructor / student notes · Algorithm definitions and fair comparison

Constructing a tour

Nearest neighbor and GRASP start at a randomly selected city. For GRASP, select an RCL size as a percentage p of the total number of cities n. The maximum RCL length is max(1, ceil(p × n / 100)), but it is capped by the number of unvisited cities. At each construction step, take that many nearest unvisited cities and choose one uniformly at random. 0% is equivalent to nearest neighbor (random distance ties); 100% chooses from all remaining cities. Random construction shuffles all cities.

Move types & iteration budget

Swap exchanges two cities; 2-opt reverses a contiguous segment; remove–insert relocates one city. One iteration is one move-selection attempt, not necessarily one neighbor evaluation. Outside SA, best examines every admissible move and chooses the largest improvement; first tries the moves in random order and accepts the first improvement; random examines every admissible move and chooses uniformly among improving moves. For 2-opt, this considers all segment lengths (2 to n−1 cities). SA instead samples a random move, with a random segment length for 2-opt. The iteration budget is shared by the selected escape methods within each run; independent runs each receive the full budget.

Combining two escape methods

Choose up to two checkboxes. Multi-start reconstructs tours at equal-budget intervals; perturbation alters the current tour at scheduled times or after stagnation. If both are selected, scheduled perturbations avoid restart boundaries. New starts or perturbations clear the tabu list and reset the VNS move-type index. SA temperature resets to T₀ each time multi-start constructs a new initial tour; the iteration budget is shared within each run; every independent run receives the full budget.

Strict city-based Tabu Search

The tabu slider limits the FIFO list to ceil(percentage × number of cities), with 0% allowing an empty list. Swap and 2-opt add both selected city IDs; remove–insert adds the relocated city. A move involving a tabu city is never eligible (no exceptions). Alone or with VNS, TS only permits strictly improving moves, using the student's best / first / random improvement rule.

SA, VNS, and hybrid behavior

Standalone SA (including SA + TS) randomly samples a permissible move; a positive change Δ may be accepted with probability exp(−Δ/T), where k counts iterations since the latest start and T = T₀ × decayk. With SA + VNS, each iteration independently chooses one of the two selected move types with equal probability, then samples one random move and applies SA acceptance. The move-type order and improvement strategy are not used in this hybrid. VNS without SA cycles between move types in the specified order, returning to the first after an improvement, without shaking.

Benchmarks & interpretation

Instance costs use TSPLIB EUC_2D rounded Euclidean distances; published TSPLIB and exact classroom optima are supplied. The notebook's best / average / worst summarize the best tour found within each independent run. All methods retain the global best even if SA or diversification increases the current objective. Different move strategies may evaluate vastly different numbers of neighbors per iteration.
